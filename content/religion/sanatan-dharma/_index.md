**Sanātana Dharma** ([Devanagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari "Devanagari"): सनातन धर्म, meaning "eternal _[dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma "Dharma")_ ", or "eternal order") [^1] or **Sanatanism** [^2] is an alternative term for [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), primarily used as an [endonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym "Endonym") (native name) to the [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym "Exonym") (foreign name) of Hinduism. The term is found in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") and other [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India "Languages of India") languages.[^3] [^4]

The term denotes the "eternal" or absolute set of duties or religiously ordained practices incumbent upon all Hindus, regardless of class, caste, or sect.[^1] Many Hindus in the Indian subcontinent call themselves Sanatanis, that is, those who follow the 'eternal dharma', to evoke a certain homogeneity in Hinduism. Its use to signify Hinduism as a religion was popularised since the 19th century by champions of Hindu orthodoxy such as [Pandit Shraddha Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shardha_Ram_Phillauri "Shardha Ram Phillauri") in reaction to [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_India "Christianity in India") and Hindu reformers such as Arya Samaj and Brahmo Samaj. Aside from its use in socio-religious contexts, it also sustains a political necessity for many Hindus.[^5]

## Etymology

In [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"), _Sanātana Dharma_ translates approximately to "eternal law" or, less literally, "eternal way." [^6] In [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali "Pali"), the equivalent term is _Dhammo Sanātano_ (धम्मो सनन्तनो).[^6]

_[Dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma "Dharma")_ is often translated as "duty" but has a deeper meaning. The word comes from the Sanskrit root " _dhṛ_ " ([धृ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A4%A7%E0%A5%83#Sanskrit "wikt:धृ")) which means "to sustain" or "that which is integral to something" (e.g., _dharma_ of sugar is to be sweet, fire to be hot). A person's _dharma_ consists of duties that sustain them according to their innate characteristics which are both spiritual and material, generating two corresponding types:[^7]

1. _Sanatana-dharma_ – duties performed according to one's spiritual (constitutional) identity as _[atman](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80tman_(Hinduism)> "Ātman (Hinduism)")_ (Self) and are thus the same for everyone. General duties include virtues such as honesty, refraining from injuring living beings, purity, goodwill, mercy, patience, forbearance, self-restraint, generosity, and asceticism.[^1]
2. _[Varnashrama-dharma](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_(Hinduism)> "Varna (Hinduism)")_ (a.k.a. Svadharma) – duties performed according to one's material (conditional) [nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%E1%B9%87a "Guṇa") and are specific to the individual at that particular time. One's "own duty" according to his or her class or varna and stage of life should win when in conflict with _Sanatana-dharma_ (e.g., A warrior injuring others as explained in _[Bhagavad Gita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavad_Gita "Bhagavad Gita")_).[^1]

According to the notion of _sanatana-dharma_, the eternal and intrinsic inclination of the living entity (_atman_) is to perform _[seva](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seva_(Indian_religions)> "Seva (Indian religions)")_ (service). _Sanatana-dharma_, being transcendental, refers to universal and axiomatic laws that are beyond our temporary belief systems.[^7]

## History

The phrase _dharma sanātana_ occurs in classical [Sanskrit literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_literature "Sanskrit literature"), for example, in the _[Manusmrti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manusmrti "Manusmrti")_ (4-138) [^8] (c. 1st – 3rd century CE) and in the _[Bhagavata Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavata_Purana "Bhagavata Purana")_ [^9] [^10] (c. 8th – 10th century CE).

In the late 19th century, the term was revived during the [Hindu revivalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_revivalism "Hindu revivalism") movement as a name for [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") as a religion in order to avoid having to use the [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym "Exonym") " [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") " which is of non-native [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language "Persian language") origin.[^11] [^12]

Today, Sanatana Dharma is associated with [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism").[^3] In current-day usage, the term _sanatana dharma_ is diminished and used to emphasize a "traditional” or _sanatani_ ("eternalist") outlook in contrast to the socio-political Hinduism embraced by movements such as the [Arya Samaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arya_Samaj "Arya Samaj").[^13] [^14] [^15] In sharp contrast to the efforts by Lahore Sanatana Dharma Sabha to preserve the Hindu tradition against the onslaught of reform, now it is being stressed that Sanatana Dharma cannot be rigid, it has to be inclusive without excluding the best and totality of knowledge to guide the karmic process, especially as Sanatana has no beginning and no end.[^16]

Reformists such as the [Arya Samaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arya_Samaj "Arya Samaj"), the [Radha Soamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radha_Soami "Radha Soami") and the [Ramakrishna Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakrishna_Mission "Ramakrishna Mission") have competed for adherents for more than a century, sometimes creating deep schisms in Hindu society, as in the case of [South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa "South Africa") Hindus who were split between the Arya Samaj and Sanatanis.[^17] While the reformist groups were better organized initially, by the 1860s, a process of internal counter-reform was underway in Sanatani groups as well, and societies to propagate orthodox beliefs along modern lines emerged, such as Sanatana Dharma Rakshini Sabha in 1873.[^18] [^19] [^20]
