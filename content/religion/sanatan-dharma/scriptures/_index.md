**Hindu texts** or **Hindu scriptures** are [manuscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuscripts "Manuscripts") and voluminous historical literature which are related to any of the diverse traditions within [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"). Some of the major [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus "Hindus") texts include the [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas "Vedas"), the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads "Upanishads"), and the [Itihasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itihasa "Itihasa"). Scholars hesitate in defining the term "Hindu scriptures" given the diverse nature of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"),[^3] [^4] but many list the [Agamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80gama_\(Hinduism\) "Āgama (Hinduism)") as Hindu scriptures,[^3] [^4] [^5] and Dominic Goodall includes [Bhagavata Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavata_Purana "Bhagavata Purana") and [Yajnavalkya Smriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yajnavalkya_Smriti "Yajnavalkya Smriti") in the list of Hindu scriptures as well.[^3]

## History

There are two historic classifications of [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") texts: *[Shruti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Aruti "Śruti")* ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"): श्रुति, [IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAST "IAST"): *Śruti*) – that which is heard,[^6] and *[Smriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smriti "Smriti")* ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"): स्मृति, [IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAST "IAST"): *Smṛti*) – that which is remembered.[^7] The *Shruti* texts refer to the body of most authoritative and ancient [religious texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_text "Religious text"), believed to be eternal knowledge authored neither by human nor divine agent but transmitted by sages (*[rishis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishis "Rishis")*). These comprise the central canon of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism").[^6] [^8] It includes the four [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas "Vedas") including its four types of embedded texts – the [Samhitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhita "Samhita"), the [Brahmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmana "Brahmana"), the [Aranyakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranyaka "Aranyaka") and the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads "Upanishads").[^9] Of the *Shrutis*, the Upanishads alone are widely influential among Hindus, considered scriptures par excellence of Hinduism, and their central ideas have continued to influence its thoughts and traditions.[^10] [^11]

The *Smriti* texts are a specific body of [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") texts attributed to an author,[^9] as a derivative work they are considered less authoritative than *Shruti* in Hinduism.[^7] The Smriti literature is a vast corpus of diverse texts, and includes but is not limited to [Vedāngas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanga "Vedanga"), the Hindu epics (such as the Mahabharat and Ramayan), the [Sutras and Shastras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmasutras "Dharmasutras"), the texts of [Hindu philosophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_philosophy "Hindu philosophy"), the [Puranas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas "Puranas"), the Kāvya or poetical literature, the *Bhasyas*, and numerous *Nibandhas* (digests) covering politics, ethics, culture, arts and society.[^12] [^13]

Many ancient Hindu texts were composed in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") and other regional Indian languages. In modern times, most ancient texts have been translated into other [Indian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India "Languages of India") and some in non-Indian languages.[^3] Prior to the start of the common era, the Hindu texts were composed orally, then memorized and transmitted orally, from one generation to the next, for more than a millennium before they were written down into manuscripts.[^14] [^15] This verbal tradition of preserving and transmitting Hindu texts, from one generation to next, continued into the modern era.[^14] [^15]

## Shruti

The Shruti texts, defined as "that which is heard", are texts that are believed to be divine revelations of God and were heard by ancient rishis thousands of years ago. Thus, an author is not attributed to these texts.[^16] The origin language of these texts is Sanskrit.[^16] The Vedas are considered Shruti texts. The Vedas consist of four parts: [Rig Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda "Rigveda"), [Sama Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaveda "Samaveda"), [Yajur Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yajurveda "Yajurveda"), and [Atharva Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atharvaveda "Atharvaveda").[^17] Each Veda is subcategorized into [Samhitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhita "Samhita"), [Brahmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmana "Brahmana"), [Aranyakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranyaka "Aranyaka"), and [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads "Upanishads").[^17]

### Vedas

Manuscripts of 18th-century Hindu texts in Sanskrit(Devanagari) and Odia.

The four Vedas ([Rig Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda "Rigveda"), [Sama Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaveda "Samaveda"), [Yajur Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yajurveda "Yajurveda"), and [Atharva Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atharvaveda "Atharvaveda")) are a large body of Hindu texts originating from the [Vedic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_period "Vedic period") in northern India, the [Rig Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rig_Veda "Rig Veda") being composed c. 1200 BCE, and its [Samhita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhita "Samhita") and [Brahmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmana "Brahmana") complete before about 800 BCE.[^18] Composed in [Vedic Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_Sanskrit "Vedic Sanskrit") [hymns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India#History "Music of India"), the texts constitute the oldest layer of [Sanskrit literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_literature "Sanskrit literature") and the oldest scriptures of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism").[^19] [^20] [^21] Hindus consider the Vedas to be timeless revelation,[^18] *[apauruṣeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apauru%E1%B9%A3eya "Apauruṣeya")*, which means "not of a man, superhuman" [^22] and "impersonal, authorless".[^23] [^24] [^25] The knowledge in the Vedas is believed in Hinduism to be eternal, uncreated, neither authored by human nor by divine source, but seen, heard and transmitted by sages.[^8]

Vedas are also called *[shruti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Aruti "Śruti")* ("what is heard") literature,[^26] distinguishing them from other religious texts, which are called **[smṛti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sm%E1%B9%9Bti "Smṛti")** ("what is remembered"). The Vedas, for orthodox Indian theologians, are considered revelations, some way or other the work of the [Deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deity "Deity"). In the Hindu Epic the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata"), the creation of Vedas is credited to the deity responsible for creation, [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma "Brahma").[^27]

Each of the four Vedas [^28] [^29] have been subclassified into four major text types:

1. the [Samhitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhita "Samhita") (mantras and benedictions),
2. the [Aranyakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranyakas "Aranyakas") (text on rituals, ceremonies, sacrifices and symbolic-sacrifices),
3. the [Brahmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmanas "Brahmanas") (commentaries on rituals, ceremonies and sacrifices), and
4. the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads "Upanishads") (text discussing meditation, philosophy and spiritual knowledge).[^28] [^30] [^31]

### Upanishads

The Upanishads are a collection of Hindu texts which contain the central philosophical concepts of Hinduism.[^10] [^1] The Upanishads are the foundation of Hindu philosophical thought and its diverse traditions.[^11] [^32]

The Upanishads are commonly referred to as *[Vedānta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ved%C4%81nta "Vedānta")*, interpreted to mean either the "last chapters, parts of the [Veda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas "Vedas") " or "the object, the highest purpose of the Veda".[^33] The concepts of [Brahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahman "Brahman") (Ultimate Reality) and [Ātman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80tman_\(Hinduism\) "Ātman (Hinduism)") (Soul, Self) are central ideas in all the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishad "Upanishad"),[^34] [^35] and "Know your Ātman" their thematic focus.[^35] The central ideas of the Upanishads have had a lasting influence on Hindu philosophy.[^10] [^11] [^2]

More than 200 Upanishads are known, of which ten are the oldest and most important and are referred to as the *[mukhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhya "Mukhya")* (main) or [principal Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Upanishads "Principal Upanishads").[^36] [^37] The ten mukhya Upanishads are: [Isha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isha_Upanishad "Isha Upanishad"), [Kena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kena_Upanishad "Kena Upanishad"), [Katha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katha_Upanishad "Katha Upanishad"), [Prashna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prashna_Upanishad "Prashna Upanishad"), [Mundaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundaka_Upanishad "Mundaka Upanishad"), [Mandukya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandukya_Upanishad "Mandukya Upanishad"), [Taittiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taittiriya_Upanishad "Taittiriya Upanishad"), [Aitareya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitareya_Upanishad "Aitareya Upanishad"), [Chandogya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandogya_Upanishad "Chandogya Upanishad"), and [Brihadaranyaka.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brihadaranyaka_Upanishad "Brihadaranyaka Upanishad")[^38] The *mukhya* Upanishads are found mostly in the concluding part of the *[Brahmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmanas "Brahmanas")* and *[Aranyakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranyakas "Aranyakas")* [^39] and were, for centuries, memorized by each generation and passed down [verbally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_tradition "Oral tradition"). The early Upanishads all predate the Common Era, some in all likelihood pre-Buddhist (6th century BCE),[^40] down to the [Maurya period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_period "Maurya period").[^41] Of the remainder, some 95 Upanishads are part of the [Muktika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muktika "Muktika") canon, composed from about the start of common era through [medieval Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hinduism "History of Hinduism"). New Upanishads, beyond the 108 in the Muktika canon, continued being composed through the early modern and modern era, though often dealing with subjects unconnected to Hinduism.[^42] [^43]

## Smriti

Smriti is the classification of literature which includes various scriptures and *Itihasa* s (epics like the [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana") and [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")), [Harivamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harivamsa "Harivamsa") [Puranas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas "Puranas"), [Agamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80gama_\(Hinduism\) "Āgama (Hinduism)") and [Darshanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darshana "Darshana"). This genre of texts includes the Sutras and Shastras of the six schools of [Hindu philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_philosophy "Hindu philosophy"): [Samkhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samkhya "Samkhya"), Yoga, [Nyaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyaya "Nyaya"), Vaisheshika, Mimamsa and [Vedanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanta "Vedanta").[^44] [^45]

The Sutras and Shastras texts were compilations of technical or specialized knowledge in a defined area. The earliest are dated to the latter half of the 1st millennium BCE. The [Dharma-shastras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmashastra "Dharmashastra") (law books), derivatives of the [Dharma-sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmasutras "Dharmasutras"). Other examples were *bhautikashastra* "physics", *[rasayanashastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasayana "Rasayana")* "chemistry", *jīvashastra* "biology", *[vastushastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vastu_shastra "Vastu shastra")* "architectural science", *[shilpashastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilpa_Shastras "Shilpa Shastras")* "science of sculpture", *arthashastra* "economics" and *nītishastra* "political science".[^46] It also includes [Tantras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantra "Tantra") and [Agama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%80gama_\(Hinduism\) "Āgama (Hinduism)") literature.[^47]

### Puranas

The Puranas, which mean "history" or "old", are Sanskrit texts which were composed between 3rd century BCE and 1000 CE.[^48] The Puranas are a vast genre of Hindu texts that encyclopedically cover a wide range of topics, particularly legends and other traditional lore.[^49] Composed primarily in Sanskrit, but also in regional languages,[^50] [^51] several of these texts are named after major [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu "Hindu") deities such as [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu "Vishnu"), [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva "Shiva"), and [Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadevi "Mahadevi").[^52] [^53]

The Puranic literature is encyclopedic,[^54] and it includes diverse topics such as [cosmogony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmogony "Cosmogony"), [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology "Cosmology"), genealogies of gods, goddesses, kings, heroes, sages, and demigods, folk tales, pilgrimages, temples, medicine, astronomy, grammar, mineralogy, humor, love stories, as well as theology and philosophy.[^49] [^51] [^52] The content is diverse across the Puranas, and each Purana has survived in numerous manuscripts which are themselves voluminous and comprehensive. The Hindu Puranas are anonymous texts and likely the work of many authors over the centuries; in contrast, most Jaina Puranas can be dated and their authors assigned.[^50]

There are 18 *Maha Puranas* (Great Puranas): [Agni Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agni_Purana "Agni Purana"), [Bhagavata Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavata_Purana "Bhagavata Purana"), [Bhavishya Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavishya_Purana "Bhavishya Purana"), [Brahmanda Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmanda_Purana "Brahmanda Purana"), [Brahmavaivarta Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma_Vaivarta_Purana "Brahma Vaivarta Purana"), [Garuda Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda_Purana "Garuda Purana"), [Kurma Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurma_Purana "Kurma Purana"), [Linga Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linga_Purana "Linga Purana"), [Markandeya Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markandeya_Purana "Markandeya Purana"), [Naradiya Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naradiya_Purana "Naradiya Purana"), [Padma Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Purana "Padma Purana"), [Shiva Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Purana "Shiva Purana"), [Skanda Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanda_Purana "Skanda Purana"), [Vamana Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vamana_Purana "Vamana Purana"), [Varaha Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varaha_Purana "Varaha Purana"), [Vayu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayu_Purana "Vayu Purana"), and [Vishnu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu_Purana "Vishnu Purana") [^48] and 18 *Upa Puranas* (Minor Puranas),[^55] with over 400,000 verses.[^49] The Puranas do not enjoy the authority of a scripture in Hinduism,[^55] but are considered a [Smriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smriti "Smriti").[^56] These Hindu texts have been influential in the [Hindu culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_culture "Hindu culture"), inspiring major national and regional annual festivals of Hinduism.[^57] The [Bhagavata Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavata_Purana "Bhagavata Purana") has been among the most celebrated and popular text in the Puranic genre.[^58] [^59] The Bhagavata Purana emphasizes bhakti (devotion) towards Krishna. The Bhagavata Purana is a key text in Krishna bhakti literature.[^48] [^60]

### Mahabharata

The *Mahābhārata*, which translates to " *The Great Indian Tale* ", was probably compiled between the 3rd century BCE and the 3rd century CE, with the oldest preserved parts not much older than around 400 BCE.[^61] [^62] The text probably reached its final form by the early [Gupta period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") (c. 4th century CE).[^63] [^64] The composition is traditionally attributed to [Vedavyasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedavyasa "Vedavyasa") and is made up of over 100,000 [shlokas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shloka "Shloka").[^65]

The plot of the Mahabharata covers the events of the war between two groups of cousins (the [Pandavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandava "Pandava") and the [Kauravas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaurava "Kaurava")) and the aftermath of the [Kurukshetra War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukshetra_War "Kurukshetra War").[^65] The Mahabharata also teaches about [dharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma "Dharma") (duty), the stories of many key figures in Hinduism, and includes the [Bhagavad Gita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavad_Gita "Bhagavad Gita").[^17]

The Bhagavad Gita is made up of 700 shlokas and is the discussion between [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna "Krishna") and [Arjuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjuna "Arjuna") on the battlefield before the start of the war. Krishna in the Bhagavad Gita teaches Arjuna about atma (soul), God, [moksha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moksha "Moksha"), and dharma.[^66]

### Ramayana

The Ramayana, an ancient Sanskrit text with the earliest part believed to have been composed in the 5th century BCE, is attributed to the sage [Valmiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiki "Valmiki") and contains over 24,000 verses.[^67]

The epic covers the life journey of [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama "Rama"), an incarnation of Vishnu, along with his wife [Sita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sita "Sita"), and brother [Lakshmana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshmana "Lakshmana"). Central to the plot is the fourteen-year exile endured by Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana, during which Sita is abducted by [Ravana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravana "Ravana") of [Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanka "Lanka"). Rama, accompanied by Lakshmana, [Hanuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman "Hanuman") (a devotee of Rama), and an army, engages in a battle with Ravana, ultimately emerging victorious with Rama's slaying of Ravana. The epic concludes with Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana's return to [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya_\(Ramayana\) "Ayodhya (Ramayana)"), where Rama is crowned king and reigns over Ayodhya.[^17]

## Historical significance

The Hindu scriptures provide the early documented history of arts and science forms in India such as [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India#History "Music of India"), [dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_in_India#Origin_of_Dance_in_India "Dance in India"), [sculptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture_in_South_Asia "Sculpture in South Asia"), [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_India#600_BCE%E2%80%94250_CE "Architecture of India"), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_astronomy "Indian astronomy"), [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanga "Vedanga"), [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_Mathematics "Vedic Mathematics"), [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda "Ayurveda") and [wellness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga#Vedic_period_\(1700%E2%80%93500_BCE\) "Yoga"). [Valmiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiki "Valmiki") 's [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana") (500 BCE to 100 BCE) mentions music and singing by [Gandharvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandharva "Gandharva"), dance by [Apsaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsara "Apsara") such as [Urvashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urvashi "Urvashi"), [Rambha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambha_\(apsara\) "Rambha (apsara)"), [Menaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menaka "Menaka"), [Tilottama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilottama "Tilottama") [Panchāpsaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panch%C4%81psaras "Panchāpsaras"), and by [Ravana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravana "Ravana") 's wives who excelling in *nrityageeta* or "singing and dancing" and *nritavaditra* or "playing musical instruments").[^95] The evidence of earliest dance related texts are in *Natasutras*, which are mentioned in the text of [Panini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81%E1%B9%87ini "Pāṇini"), the sage who wrote the classic on [Sanskrit grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyakarana "Vyakarana"), and who is dated to about 500 BCE.[^96] [^97] This performance arts related [Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutra "Sutra") text is mentioned in other late Vedic texts, as are two scholars names Shilalin ([IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAST "IAST"): Śilālin) and Krishashva (Kṛśaśva), credited to be pioneers in the studies of ancient drama, singing, dance and Sanskrit compositions for these arts.[^96] [^98] Richmond et al. estimate the *Natasutras* to have been composed around 600 BCE, whose complete manuscript has not survived into the modern age.[^96] [^97]
