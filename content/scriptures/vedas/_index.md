> [!abstract] TL;DR: The Vedas
> Ancient Sanskrit scriptures (1500-500 BCE) forming [[Hinduism]]'s foundation. Four texts: [[scriptures/vedas/_index | Rigveda]] (hymns), [[Yajurveda]] (rituals), [[Samaveda]] (music), [[Atharvaveda]] (practical knowledge). Each has four layers: [[Samhitas]] (mantras) → [[Brahmanas]] (ritual explanations) → [[Aranyakas]] (symbolic interpretations) → [[Upanishads]] (philosophy introducing [[Brahman]]/[[Atman]]). Transmitted orally for 1000+ years using sophisticated mnemonic techniques. Divides Indian philosophy into orthodox ([[Āstika]]) schools accepting Vedic authority vs heterodox ([[Nāstika]]) like [[Buddhism]]/[[Jainism]] rejecting it. Remains central to Hindu identity and global spiritual thought.

## Core Definition & Significance

The **Vedas** are ancient Sanskrit texts forming the foundational scriptures of [[Hinduism]]. The term derives from the Sanskrit root _vid-_ meaning "to know," making Vedas literally "knowledge."^[[Monier-Williams 1899, p. 1015]] ^[[Apte 1965, p. 856]] These texts constitute the oldest layer of Sanskrit literature and represent humanity's earliest preserved religious writings.^[[Radhakrishnan & Moore 1957, p. 3; Witzel 2003, p. 68; MacDonell 2004, pp. 29–39]]

## The Four Vedas

1. **[[Rigveda]]** - Collection of 1,028 hymns and 10,600 verses dedicated to various deities^[[Avari 2007, p. 77]]
2. **[[Yajurveda]]** - Prose mantras for ritual sacrifices^[[Witzel 2003, pp. 76–77]]
3. **[[Samaveda]]** - Musical arrangements, mostly derived from Rigveda^[[Witzel 1997, pp. 269–270]]
4. **[[Atharvaveda]]** - 760 hymns including practical knowledge and rituals^[[Michaels 2004, p. 56]]

## Four-Layer Structure

Each Veda contains four types of texts:^[[Gavin Flood (1996), An Introduction to Hinduism, Cambridge University Press, ISBN 978-0-521-43878-0, pp. 35–39]]

### [[Samhitas]] (Collections)

- Core mantras and hymns
- Oldest layer (c. 1500-900 BCE)^[[It is certain that the hymns of the Rig Veda post-date Indo-Iranian separation of ca. 2000 BCE]]
- Ritual invocations to deities

### [[Brahmanas]] (Commentaries)

- Prose explanations of rituals
- Dated 900-700 BCE^[[Michael Witzel, "Tracing the Vedic dialects"]] ^[[Biswas et al (1989), Cosmic Perspectives, Cambridge University Press, pp. 42–43]]
- Practical guidance for ceremonies

### [[Aranyakas]] (Forest Texts)

- Symbolic interpretations of rituals
- Bridge between ritual and philosophy^[[Jan Gonda (1975), Vedic Literature, pp. 424–426]]

### [[Upanishads]] (Philosophical Texts)

- Abstract philosophical speculation
- Foundation of Hindu philosophy^[[Wendy Doniger (1990), Textual Sources for the Study of Hinduism, pp. 2–3]] ^[[Wiman Dissanayake (1993), Self as Body in Asian Theory and Practice, p. 39]]
- Core concepts: [[Brahman]] (Ultimate Reality) and [[Atman]] (Individual Self)^[[Mahadevan 1952, p. 59]] ^[[PT Raju (1985), Structural Depths of Indian Thought, pp. 35–36]]

## Historical Context & Dating

**Composition Period:** c. 1500-500 BCE^[[Philological estimates tend to date the bulk of the text to the second half of the second millennium]]

- **Rigveda:** 1500-1200 BCE (Punjab region)^[[Flood 1996, p. 37]] ^[[Witzel 1995, p. 4]]
- **Other Samhitas:** 1200-900 BCE ([[Kuru Kingdom]])^[[Witzel 2003, p. 69]]
- **Later texts:** Extended through 500 BCE^[[Witzel 2003, p. 68]]

**Geographic Origin:** Northwestern Indian subcontinent, spreading eastward to Ganges region^[[Flood 1996, p. 37]] ^[[Witzel 1995, p. 4]]

## Unique Characteristics

### Oral Transmission

The Vedas were transmitted orally for over 1,000 years before being written down.^[[Wood 2007]] ^[[Witzel 2003, p. 69]] This preservation used sophisticated mnemonic techniques including:^[[Wood 2007]] ^[[Hexam 2011, chapter 8]] ^[[Dwyer 2013]]

- Eleven different recitation modes
- Physical movements synchronized with sounds
- Group chanting for accuracy verification
- Hand gestures ([[mudras]]) for visual confirmation^[[Wilke & Moebus 2011, p. 479]]

### Sacred Authority ([[Śruti]] vs [[Smṛti]])

- **Śruti ("what is heard"):** Vedas - considered divine revelation^[[Apte 1965, p. 887]]
- **Smṛti ("what is remembered"):** Later texts like epics and law books^[[Apte 1965, p. 887]]
- Vedas viewed as _[[apauruṣeya]]_ - "not of human authorship"^[[Apte 1965, "apauruSeya"]] ^[[Todd 2013, p. 128]]

## Philosophical Impact

### Orthodox vs Heterodox Schools

**Orthodox ([[Āstika]])** - Accept Vedic authority:^[[Elisa Freschi (2012): "The Vedas are recognized as a deontological epistemic authority by a Hindu orthodox school"]] ^[["astika" and "nastika". Encyclopædia Britannica Online, 2016]]

- Six classical Hindu philosophical schools
- Acknowledge Vedas as valid knowledge source

**Heterodox ([[Nāstika]])** - Reject Vedic authority:^[[Flood 1996, p. 82]] ^[["astika" and "nastika". Encyclopædia Britannica Online, 2016]]

- [[Buddhism]], [[Jainism]], [[Charvaka]]
- Developed independent philosophical systems

### Key Concepts Introduced

- **[[Brahman]]:** Ultimate cosmic reality
- **[[Atman]]:** Individual consciousness/soul
- **[[Karma]]:** Action and consequence
- **[[Dharma]]:** Righteous duty
- **[[Rita]]/[[Rta]]:** Cosmic order^[[Mookerji 2011, p. 36]]

### [[Vedangas]] (Auxiliary Sciences)

Six supporting disciplines developed to understand Vedas:^[[James Lochtefeld (2002), "Vedanga" in The Illustrated Encyclopedia of Hinduism, pp. 744–745]] ^[[Wilke & Moebus 2011, pp. 391–394]]

1. **[[Śikṣā]]** - Phonetics
2. **[[Chandas]]** - Prosody
3. **[[Vyākaraṇa]]** - Grammar
4. **[[Nirukta]]** - Etymology
5. **[[Kalpa]]** - Ritual procedures
6. **[[Jyotiṣa]]** - Astronomy/astrology

## Key Takeaways

1. **Historical Importance:** Oldest preserved religious literature, foundational to Hindu civilization
2. **Structural Complexity:** Four-layer system progressing from ritual to philosophy
3. **Transmission Method:** Remarkable oral preservation demonstrating ancient mnemonic mastery
4. **Philosophical Foundation:** Core concepts continue influencing spiritual thought globally
5. **Living Tradition:** Continues active use in religious practices and scholarly study

The Vedas represent humanity's earliest systematic attempt to understand cosmic order, divine reality, and human purpose - making them invaluable for understanding both ancient Indian civilization and the development of religious thought worldwide.
